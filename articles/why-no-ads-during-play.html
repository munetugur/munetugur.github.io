Why snackplay keeps ads out of the way while you're playing

snackplay runs on ads — that's the whole business model, and we're upfront about it. But where those ads go matters, especially in a game like Asteroid Dash where the entire screen is one tap zone for moving your ship.

The problem with ads during gameplay

If a banner or button sits anywhere near the play area of a game built around rapid tapping, players will hit it by accident — sometimes a lot. That's a bad experience for the player, and it's also against the advertising policies we operate under: ad placements that are likely to produce accidental clicks aren't allowed, for good reason.

Where ads actually go

Our plan is to keep ad placements to moments where the game has already stopped — the result screen after a run ends, for instance — rather than anywhere inside active play. It's a smaller number of ad opportunities than we could technically fit in, and we're keeping it that way deliberately.

The tradeoff we're making

This almost certainly means lower revenue per session than a more aggressive layout would produce. We think it's worth it: a game that respects a thirty-second attention span is also a game people are willing to open again tomorrow, and repeat visits matter more to this business long-term than squeezing extra impressions out of a single session.
