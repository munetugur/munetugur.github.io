Dev diary · 2026-08-28

Why we're running on a free URL, not a paid domain

This site lives at a free GitHub Pages address instead of a domain we bought. That was a deliberate call, not a temporary placeholder we forgot to fix.

The case against spending upfront

This project hasn't earned a cent yet. Buying a domain — even a cheap one — means spending real money on a business that hasn't proven it can make any back. That's a bet we didn't want to make before we had to.

Does a free URL hurt ad approval?

Somewhat, yes — sites on free subdomains generally have a harder time getting approved for ad programs than sites on their own domain. But it's not a wall: GitHub Pages specifically has a track record of sites getting approved, unlike some other free hosts. Given that, the honest trade was: accept a bit more friction now, in exchange for spending zero dollars before there's any revenue to justify it.

What happens if it starts working

The plan is simple: the day this site earns enough to comfortably cover a domain, we buy one and switch over. No speculative spending, no debt against a business that might not work out — just reinvesting money the site has actually made. If it never gets there, we haven't lost anything on hosting.
