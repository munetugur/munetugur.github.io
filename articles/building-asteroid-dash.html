Building Asteroid Dash: a game you can finish at a bus stop

The first version of this game wasn't a space shooter at all — it was an idle/clicker game about growing a bonsai tree. Tap to water it, buy upgrades, watch it grow over days. On paper it looked reasonable: idle games are one of the most durable genres on the web.

It was the wrong genre for what we actually wanted. Idle games are built around leaving them running and checking back later — that's the entire appeal. What we wanted was the opposite: something you open once, play for under a minute, and either put down or immediately try again. A game for the thirty seconds between things, not a game that asks you to come back tomorrow.

What we built instead

Asteroid Dash is about as simple as a shooter gets: your ship sits near the bottom of the screen, moves left and right, and fires automatically. Asteroids fall from the top. One hit ends the run. You get a score, you see how close you got to your best, and you can restart with a single tap.

That "one hit and it's over" rule was a deliberate choice. It would have been easy to give the ship three lives or a shield meter, but that adds a decision the player has to track. One life keeps the whole game legible at a glance — you're either flying or you're not.

Designed for all ages, on purpose

There's no story, no characters, and nothing that reads as violent — you're breaking up rocks, not fighting anything alive. That was intentional from the first design doc: we wanted a game that's fine for a wide age range without watering down the core loop.
