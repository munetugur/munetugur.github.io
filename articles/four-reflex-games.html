Dev diary · 2026-08-28

Four reflex games, four different gaps in your day

We shipped four games in one push, and all four are, broadly, reflex tests. That wasn't laziness — reflex mechanics are some of the most reliable in casual gaming, and there's real room to differentiate inside that category by tuning what exactly you're reacting to.

Quick Draw — pure reaction time, ~10-15 seconds

No obstacle, no scoring beyond a stopwatch: wait for the screen to turn green, tap, see your time in milliseconds across three rounds. This is the shortest thing on the site on purpose — it's built for a gap so small that opening a game at all seems excessive.

Mole Rush — targeting under pressure, ~20 seconds

The classic whack-a-mole loop, fixed at a 20-second timer. The twist is a rare gold mole worth 5x points that appears for less time than a regular one — it rewards paying attention to the whole board, not just reacting to the last thing that popped up.

Flap — sustained reflexes, ~15-45 seconds

The flap-through-gaps genre lives or dies on how the physics feel, so we spent the most tuning time here. The original addition is a near-miss bonus: thread a gap within 20 pixels of the edge and you get an extra point, which nudges you to fly tight instead of playing it safe in the middle.

Beat Match — timing against a rhythm, ~30-60 seconds

This one's structurally closest to Pancake Tower — tap-on-timing — but the feel is completely different because the cue is a falling note against a fixed line rather than a swinging block. No music yet (that's a real gap, not a design choice), but the timing windows work the same way a rhythm game's would: tight for "Perfect," forgiving for "Good."

All four, plus the rest of the lineup: back to snackplay.
