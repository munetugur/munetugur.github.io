Fruit Drop, Pattern Panic, and Word Dash: three games that aren't about reflexes

Most of what we've built leans on reaction speed. These three don't — they're about a small amount of planning, memory, or recall instead, and they fill the longer end of our lineup (1 to 4 minutes) as a result.

Fruit Drop — merge-and-chain, ~1-2 minutes

Tap a column, drop a fruit, and matching fruit directly below it merges into the next size up. The interesting decision is entirely about which column — dropping into the wrong one wastes a merge opportunity, and setting up a chain (a merge that immediately causes another merge above it) is worth substantially more than the same two merges done separately.

Pattern Panic — Simon-says with rising tempo, ~1-3 minutes

Watch a sequence of four colored panels light up, then repeat it back. Each round adds one more step to the sequence, and the playback gets faster as you go, so the game's difficulty comes entirely from your working memory, not from anything mechanical. Score is just how many rounds you survive.

Word Dash — typing under a clock, 30 seconds fixed

The one DOM-based game on the site instead of canvas, because it needed a real text field to bring up a keyboard on mobile. Words start short and get longer the more you get right in a row, so it adapts to how fast you actually type instead of having one fixed difficulty.
